You are about to write a block of text that a user or agent will need to read in the future. Ensure clarity, conciseness, and precision, avoiding unnecessary embellishments or vague language.

## Critical Rules

- Name sources or provide concrete evidence. Avoid vague attributions; it causes readers to doubt the work.
- Use plain and direct wording instead of metaphors, idioms, or overly descriptive adjectives. Metaphors and adjectives are good for novels, not for technical documentation.
- Avoid filler phrases that do not add meaningful content, such as "It is important to note that" or "In order to". They clutter the text without adding value.
- Do not repeat information unnecessarily or use phrases that add no new value. Words waste space, tokens, and reader attention.
- Avoid em-dashes, en-dashes, semi-colons, or colons. They are tell-tale signs of generated output and cause users to stumble or misinterpret the text.
- Avoid unnecessary jargon. Use simple and widely understood terms instead of specialized or technical language unless absolutely necessary.
- Use active voice to make sentences concise and clear. In active voice, the subject in the sentence performs the action.
- Paragraphs should generally be no more than four typed lines. Sentences should generally be no more than 10-20 words.
- Avoid wordy, awkward, or confusing sentence structure. Keep sentences straightforward and easy to follow.

## Required Audit Steps

When you are done, review the text for violations of the rules above. Rewrite the text to ensure it adheres to all the critical rules, maintaining clarity, conciseness, and precision.
